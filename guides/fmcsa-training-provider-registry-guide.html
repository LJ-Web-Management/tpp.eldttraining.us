The FMCSA Training Provider Registry: How It Works and How to Verify a Provider

If a driver's training isn't on the Training Provider Registry, as far as the state is concerned, it didn't happen. Here's how the TPR works and how to make sure the training you buy, recommend, or resell will actually count.

Updated October 25, 2026 3 min read By the HAZWOPER OSHA Training team, an FMCSA TPR-listed ELDT theory provider

Key Takeaways

  • The TPR is FMCSA's database of providers allowed to deliver ELDT, and the system that records each driver's completed training.
  • Providers must report completions by midnight of the second business day; states check the TPR before CDL skills tests and the Hazmat knowledge test.
  • Before buying or reselling training, confirm the provider is listed for the specific course and training type (theory or BTW).

What the Training Provider Registry is

The Training Provider Registry (TPR) is an FMCSA system that does two jobs:

  1. It lists every training provider that has certified it meets the ELDT requirements in 49 CFR Part 380, Subpart G.
  2. It stores driver training records submitted by those providers when a driver completes theory or behind-the-wheel training.

State driver licensing agencies use those records to confirm a driver has completed ELDT before testing.

Who registers

Any organization delivering ELDT must register, including CDL schools, community colleges, motor carriers that train their own drivers, school districts, government agencies, labor organizations, and online providers. Registration is per training location, and each listing shows which courses it offers and whether it provides theory, behind-the-wheel, or both.

Providers self-certify that they meet curriculum, instructor, facility, vehicle, and recordkeeping standards. FMCSA can audit providers, and it can remove providers from the registry when they can't demonstrate compliance.

How a driver's record gets to the state

  1. The driver creates a TPR account and shares their information with the provider (or the provider enters it).
  2. The driver completes training and, for theory, passes the assessment with at least 80%.
  3. The provider submits the completion record to the TPR by midnight of the second business day.
  4. FMCSA transmits the record to the driver's state licensing agency.
  5. The state confirms the record before administering the skills test (Class A, Class B, B-to-A, P, S) or the Hazmat knowledge test.

If any step breaks down, such as a provider forgetting to submit or entering the wrong license number, the driver can be turned away at the testing site.

How to verify a provider

Whether you're a driver, employer, school, or reseller, take these steps before you buy:

  • Search the TPR on FMCSA's website by provider name or location.
  • Check the training type. A provider listed only for behind-the-wheel can't report theory, and vice versa.
  • Check the specific courses. Make sure the listing includes each class or endorsement you need.
  • Ask about reporting. Confirm the provider submits completions to the TPR for you and how quickly.
  • Ask who answers questions. Drivers inevitably need help with logins, assessments, or TPR records.

Red flags: a provider that can't give you its TPR listing, offers a certificate without reporting to the TPR, guarantees a pass, or offers training that's dramatically shorter than the full curriculum.

If you resell or recommend training

Organizations that send drivers to a provider (staffing agencies, associations, consultants, or fleets) put their reputation on the line with every recommendation. A partner model where the TPR-listed provider remains the provider of record means the compliance, reporting, and recordkeeping obligations stay with the provider, while you still offer the training to your audience.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check if an ELDT provider is registered?
Search FMCSA's Training Provider Registry by provider name or location, and confirm the listing includes the training type (theory or behind-the-wheel) and the specific courses you need.
How quickly do providers report ELDT completion?
Providers must submit completion records to the Training Provider Registry by midnight of the second business day after the driver completes training.
What happens if my training isn't in the TPR?
The state licensing agency will not administer the CDL skills test (or the Hazmat knowledge test) until a completed ELDT record appears in the registry. Contact the provider to submit or correct the record.

This guide is for general information and reflects FMCSA ELDT requirements as of October 25, 2026. Regulations and state policies change; confirm current requirements with FMCSA and your state driver licensing agency before making training or compliance decisions.
