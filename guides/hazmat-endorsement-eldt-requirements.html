Hazmat Endorsement ELDT Requirements: What Drivers and Employers Need to Know

Hazmat-endorsed drivers command higher pay and open doors to fuel, chemical, and specialized freight. The good news for employers: hazmat is the one ELDT category that's theory only, which means it can be completed entirely online.

Updated October 25, 2026 3 min read By the ELDTTraining.us team at HAZWOPER OSHA Training, an FMCSA TPR-listed ELDT theory provider

Key Takeaways

  • Drivers getting the Hazardous Materials (H) endorsement for the first time must complete ELDT hazmat theory before the H knowledge test.
  • There is no behind-the-wheel requirement for hazmat, so the ELDT portion can be completed fully online.
  • Drivers also need a TSA security threat assessment, so start both processes early.

Who needs hazmat ELDT

Any driver getting the Hazardous Materials (H) endorsement for the first time must complete hazmat ELDT theory, and the state checks the Training Provider Registry before administering the hazmat knowledge test. The combined Hazmat & Tank Vehicle (X) endorsement includes the H endorsement, so first-time X applicants need hazmat theory too.

Drivers who already hold an H endorsement and are simply renewing it don't need to repeat ELDT, though they still must meet renewal and security threat assessment requirements.

What the hazmat theory curriculum covers

Appendix E of 49 CFR Part 380 lays out the hazmat theory curriculum, including:

  • Basic introductory hazmat requirements and definitions
  • Reading and using shipping papers
  • Marking, labeling, and placarding requirements
  • Loading, unloading, segregation, and securement
  • Bulk packaging and tank marking
  • Hazmat routing and driving and parking rules
  • Emergency response procedures and incident reporting
  • Security awareness and hazmat security plans
  • Special requirements for specific hazard classes

Drivers must complete the full curriculum and score at least 80% on the theory assessment.

Don't forget the TSA security threat assessment

Separate from ELDT, every hazmat endorsement applicant must pass a Transportation Security Administration (TSA) security threat assessment, which includes fingerprinting and a background check. It can take several weeks, so employers often have drivers start the TSA application and ELDT theory at the same time.

An employer's hazmat upskilling strategy

Because hazmat ELDT is theory only, it's one of the easiest credentials to add to an existing driver workforce:

  1. Identify drivers with clean records who want hazmat lanes.
  2. Have them start the TSA threat assessment.
  3. Assign hazmat ELDT theory online, completed between loads or during downtime.
  4. Once the TPR record and TSA clearance are in place, the driver takes the H knowledge test.

Remember that ELDT is entry-level training. Employers also have ongoing hazmat employee training obligations under 49 CFR Part 172, Subpart H, which is a separate requirement.

Frequently asked questions

Does the hazmat endorsement require behind-the-wheel training under ELDT?
No. The Hazardous Materials (H) endorsement requires ELDT theory only, with no behind-the-wheel component.
Do I need ELDT to renew my hazmat endorsement?
No. ELDT applies when obtaining the H endorsement for the first time. Renewals have their own requirements, including the TSA security threat assessment.
Does the X endorsement require hazmat ELDT?
Yes. The combined Hazmat & Tank Vehicle (X) endorsement includes the H endorsement, so first-time applicants need ELDT hazmat theory.

This guide is for general information and reflects FMCSA ELDT requirements as of October 25, 2026. Regulations and state policies change; confirm current requirements with FMCSA and your state driver licensing agency before making training or compliance decisions.
