ELDT for Government Fleets: A Guide for Cities, Counties, and State Agencies

Public works crews, snowplow operators, transit drivers, sanitation teams, and utility departments all depend on CDL holders. ELDT applies to public employers too, and agencies that plan for it can promote from within faster and spend less per driver.

Updated October 25, 2026 3 min read By the ELDTTraining.us team at HAZWOPER OSHA Training, an FMCSA TPR-listed ELDT theory provider

Key Takeaways

  • Government employees getting a first-time Class A or B CDL, a B-to-A upgrade, or a first-time P, S, or H endorsement need ELDT, the same as private-sector drivers.
  • Some government roles fall under CDL exemptions (such as certain military and firefighting operations), so confirm which positions actually require a CDL.
  • Agencies commonly pair online theory from a registered provider with in-house behind-the-wheel training using their own vehicles and trainers.

Does ELDT apply to government employees?

Yes. The FMCSA ELDT rule is tied to obtaining a CDL or endorsement, not to the type of employer. A city maintenance worker getting a Class B CDL to drive a dump truck must complete ELDT just like a driver at a private carrier.

Government agencies commonly need ELDT for roles like:

  • Public works and street maintenance (dump trucks, plows, sweepers)
  • Sanitation and solid waste collection
  • Water, wastewater, and utility departments
  • Parks and recreation equipment haulers
  • Public transit and paratransit (Passenger endorsement)
  • Pupil transportation (School Bus endorsement)
  • Hazmat response and fuel delivery (Hazmat endorsement)

Exemptions that may apply

Some government drivers don't need a CDL at all, which means ELDT doesn't apply. Under 49 CFR 383.3, exemptions include certain military vehicle operators and, at a state's option, firefighters and operators of emergency equipment. Some states have also created limited exemptions for specific municipal operations.

Because exemptions vary by state and by role, confirm each position's CDL requirement with your state driver licensing agency before you budget for training.

Using ELDT to promote from within

Many agencies struggle to hire experienced CDL holders at public-sector pay rates. A more reliable strategy is to train current employees, such as laborers, groundskeepers, and equipment operators, into CDL roles. ELDT makes that path more structured:

  1. The employee obtains a commercial learner's permit.
  2. They complete ELDT theory online, often during slower seasons or between assignments.
  3. Your in-house trainer delivers behind-the-wheel instruction on agency equipment.
  4. Once both portions are on the Training Provider Registry, they take the skills test.

The result is a CDL pipeline built from people who already know your agency, your equipment, and your community.

In-house vs. outsourced theory

An agency can register on the TPR and deliver its own theory instruction, but that means building and maintaining a compliant curriculum, assessments, instructor qualifications, reporting, and three years of records. Most agencies prefer to keep behind-the-wheel training in-house, where their equipment and trainers add the most value, and buy theory from a registered provider.

If your agency already uses a learning management system, look for a provider that offers a SCORM package so completions and records stay in your system.

Procurement tips for ELDT theory

  • Verify TPR listing. Ask for the provider's registry listing and confirm it covers theory for each course you need.
  • Cover every class and endorsement. A single contract covering Class A, Class B, B-to-A upgrade, P, S, and H avoids juggling vendors.
  • Ask about per-seat volume tiers. Annual seat volume across departments can unlock lower rates.
  • Require bilingual access. An English/Spanish toggle supports a broader workforce.
  • Confirm reporting and support. Who reports completions, and who answers driver questions?

Frequently asked questions

Do municipal employees need ELDT to get a CDL?
Yes. Government employees getting a first-time Class A or B CDL, a Class B to A upgrade, or a first-time Passenger, School Bus, or Hazmat endorsement must complete ELDT, unless their role is exempt from CDL requirements.
Are firefighters exempt from ELDT?
Under 49 CFR 383.3, states may exempt firefighters and emergency equipment operators from CDL requirements. If a state uses that exemption, those drivers don't need a CDL and therefore don't need ELDT. Check your state's rules.
Can a government agency deliver its own ELDT training?
Yes. Agencies can register on the FMCSA Training Provider Registry. Many deliver behind-the-wheel training in-house and use a registered online provider for theory.

This guide is for general information and reflects FMCSA ELDT requirements as of October 25, 2026. Regulations and state policies change; confirm current requirements with FMCSA and your state driver licensing agency before making training or compliance decisions.
