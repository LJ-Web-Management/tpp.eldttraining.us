ELDT Basics

What Is ELDT? FMCSA Entry-Level Driver Training Requirements Explained

Since February 7, 2022, almost every new commercial driver in the United States has had to complete federally standardized training before testing for a CDL. Here's what the ELDT rule requires, who it applies to, and what it means for the organizations that hire, train, and support CDL drivers.

Updated October 25, 2026 4 min read By the ELDTTraining.us team at HAZWOPER OSHA Training, an FMCSA TPR-listed ELDT theory provider

Key Takeaways

  • ELDT applies to drivers getting a Class A or Class B CDL for the first time, upgrading from Class B to Class A, or getting a Passenger (P), School Bus (S), or Hazardous Materials (H) endorsement for the first time.
  • Training has two parts: theory (classroom or online knowledge instruction) and behind-the-wheel (range and public road). Hazmat requires theory only.
  • Training must come from a provider listed on the FMCSA Training Provider Registry (TPR), which reports completion directly to FMCSA.
  • Theory and behind-the-wheel can be completed with different providers, which is why many organizations outsource theory online.

What ELDT is

ELDT stands for Entry-Level Driver Training. It is a set of federal minimum training standards created by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) and codified in 49 CFR Part 380, Subparts F and G. The rule took effect on February 7, 2022.

Before ELDT, there was no national standard for how a new commercial driver had to be trained. Some drivers attended months-long programs; others studied a state manual and took the test. ELDT closed that gap by requiring a consistent curriculum, delivered by a registered training provider, before a driver can sit for certain CDL tests.

Who needs ELDT training

ELDT applies when a driver is obtaining one of the following for the first time:

  • A Class A commercial driver's license
  • A Class B commercial driver's license
  • An upgrade from Class B to Class A
  • A Passenger (P) endorsement
  • A School Bus (S) endorsement
  • A Hazardous Materials (H) endorsement (which is also part of the combined Hazmat & Tank Vehicle (X) endorsement)

The requirement is tied to the test, not the job. The state driver licensing agency checks the Training Provider Registry for a completed ELDT record before administering the CDL skills test (for Class A, Class B, the B-to-A upgrade, P, and S) or the knowledge test for the H endorsement.

Tanker (N) note: The federal ELDT rule does not include a separate tanker curriculum, but tank vehicle operation is part of hazmat and Class A/B knowledge, and many employers still require structured tanker training for new drivers.

Who is not required to complete ELDT

Several groups fall outside the requirement. Always confirm specifics with your state driver licensing agency, but in general ELDT does not apply to:

  • Drivers who were issued a commercial learner's permit (CLP) before February 7, 2022 and obtain their CDL before that CLP expires.
  • Drivers who already hold the CDL class or endorsement, including those reinstating a CDL or endorsement they previously held.
  • Drivers who are exempt from CDL requirements altogether under 49 CFR 383.3, such as certain military operators, farmers, and firefighters or emergency equipment operators in states that use that exemption.
  • Veterans who qualify for a skills test waiver under the military operator program.

Part one: theory training

Theory instruction is the knowledge portion of ELDT. It can be delivered in a classroom, online, or as a mix, and it covers the topics listed in the curriculum appendices of Part 380:

  • Appendix A — Class A CDL theory (basic operation, safe operating procedures, advanced operating practices, vehicle systems and reporting malfunctions, and non-driving activities like hours of service and cargo handling)
  • Appendix B — Class B CDL theory
  • Appendix C — Passenger (P) endorsement
  • Appendix D — School Bus (S) endorsement
  • Appendix E — Hazardous Materials (H) endorsement

There is no minimum number of hours for theory instruction. Instead, the driver must cover every required topic and score at least 80% on a written or electronic assessment. That proficiency-based structure is why well-designed online courses have become the most common way drivers complete theory.

Part two: behind-the-wheel training

Behind-the-wheel (BTW) training is hands-on instruction on a range (a closed area away from public traffic) and on a public road. Like theory, there is no federal minimum number of hours; the instructor determines when the driver has demonstrated proficiency in every required skill.

The Hazardous Materials (H) endorsement has no behind-the-wheel component. It is theory only.

For a deeper comparison, see our guide to ELDT theory vs. behind-the-wheel training.

The Training Provider Registry

Only providers listed on the FMCSA Training Provider Registry (TPR) can deliver ELDT. Each provider self-certifies that it meets the curriculum, instructor, facility, and vehicle requirements, and FMCSA can audit and remove providers that don't.

When a driver completes training, the provider must submit the completion record to the TPR by midnight of the second business day. That electronic record is what the state checks before testing, so a paper certificate alone is not enough. Learn more in our Training Provider Registry guide.

What ELDT means for employers and training organizations

For anyone who recruits, employs, or supports new CDL drivers, ELDT added a required step to every new driver's path. That creates three practical questions:

  1. Who delivers theory? You can become a registered provider yourself, or use a provider that's already on the TPR.
  2. Who delivers behind-the-wheel? This usually requires vehicles, ranges, and qualified instructors, so it often stays with a CDL school or an in-house trainer.
  3. How do drivers pay, and who benefits? Theory training is a recurring purchase for every new driver, every upgrade, and every new endorsement.

That last point is why organizations like CDL schools, staffing agencies, associations, and fleets are adding ELDT theory to what they offer, either to reduce their own costs or to create a new revenue stream.

Frequently asked questions

When did the ELDT rule take effect?
The FMCSA Entry-Level Driver Training rule took effect on February 7, 2022.
Is there a minimum number of hours for ELDT theory training?
No. ELDT theory is proficiency-based. Drivers must cover every required topic and score at least 80% on the theory assessment, but there is no minimum number of classroom or online hours.
Can ELDT theory training be completed online?
Yes. FMCSA allows theory instruction to be delivered online, as long as the provider is listed on the Training Provider Registry and the course covers the required curriculum and assessment.
Do drivers who already have a CDL need ELDT?
Generally no. ELDT applies to first-time Class A or B CDLs, Class B to A upgrades, and first-time P, S, or H endorsements. Drivers who already hold the class or endorsement are not required to retake it.
Can theory and behind-the-wheel be done with different providers?
Yes. A driver can complete theory with one registered provider and behind-the-wheel with another. Each provider reports its own portion to the Training Provider Registry.

This guide is for general information and reflects FMCSA ELDT requirements as of October 25, 2026. Regulations and state policies change; confirm current requirements with FMCSA and your state driver licensing agency before making training or compliance decisions.
